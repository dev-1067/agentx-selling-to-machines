AgentX · Challenge 2 · Selling to Machines · Round 1 abstract

SAUDA · an honest, profitable seller agent

Team HackBots · Devaansh Sharma · Techfest, IIT Bombay 2026–27

The problem

Shoppers now send AI agents to buy for them, but store assistants are built for people. Research on agent-to-agent commerce finds sellers that accept below-cost deals, are anchored by fake budgets, obey fake "system" messages and deadlock.

Innovation: deciding is code, talking is checked

SAUDA never lets the conversation set the price. A strategist chooses a basket price each round from numbers only. A PolicyGuard clamps every offer to [floor, list] and stock, and refuses any bid carrying slipped-in out-of-stock, over-stock or one-rupee lines. A truth gate checks every sentence against the official truthfulness rules and a ledger of numbers genuinely in play; a failing sentence is replaced by a plain one. A trained classifier names the buyer's tactic (fake competitor quote, prompt injection, fake authority, false agreement, stock claim, threat) to choose calm wording and an audit label, never a discount. Because prices depend only on the basket and priced bids, two buyers with the same needs and bids get identical offers.

The strategist reads the buyer's opening bid to tell a near-limit opener (close fast) from a long haggle (hold the ask while it keeps conceding). It fits the buyer's concession curve to its real bids, predicts its limit, and makes the final ask there; it always closes or walks away in time, so it does not deadlock.

Results (official scoring code, hidden-set style run)

Default run, 210 negotiations: score 54.0 versus the starter baseline's 48.5, 0 policy violations, 0 untrue claims, 1 deadlocks, deal rate 76.2% (98.8% where a compliant deal existed), 0.695 ms per turn. The strategy was tuned on training seeds and selected on held-out seeds across eleven buyer styles; on the eight extra gym styles it scores 43.5 against the baseline's 48.5.

Impact

A seller that stays inside policy and truthful against manipulative machine buyers lets a small store join agentic commerce without margin leakage or compliance risk, and its outputs are auditable round by round.

Cost estimate

The seller runs on the standard library in under 2 ms per turn, so serving cost is effectively one CPU core and no model calls per message. The tactic classifier is a 1.3 MB file. Optional Rezolve TraceWare verification adds one metered call per outgoing message; at ten rounds per negotiation that is at most ten calls per deal.

Build summary and Rezolve Labs

Code: agent/ (seller, guards, classifier inference, Rezolve adapter), gym/ (eight scripted and four local-LLM buyers, tuner), model/ (data and training), dashboard/ (control room), 51 automated tests and a browser test of every dashboard button. Rezolve Labs capabilities: TraceWare commerce claim verification for the truth gate and managed knowledge retrieval for catalogue and policy. Credentials were not issued at submission; the adapter runs the local verifier and switches to TraceWare when configured, failing closed.
